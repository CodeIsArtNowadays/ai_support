Trip Report: Two Weeks in Portugal

In October 2024, I spent 14 days traveling through Portugal, starting in Lisbon and ending in Porto. The round-trip flight from New York cost $780 per person, departing from JFK on October 3rd.

I spent the first four days in Lisbon, staying at a guesthouse in the Alfama district for $65 per night. The Pastéis de Belém bakery is a must-visit — a box of six custard tarts costs around 7 euros. I took the famous Tram 28 through the old town, which costs 3 euros for a single ride, though a day pass for all public transport is 6.60 euros.

On day five, I took a train to Sintra, which takes about 40 minutes from Lisbon's Rossio station and costs 4.80 euros one way. The Pena Palace entrance fee was 14 euros, and I recommend arriving before 9:30 AM to avoid the tour bus crowds that arrive around 11 AM.

Days six through nine were spent in the Algarve region, specifically in Lagos. I rented a car for five days at a cost of 180 euros total, which made exploring the coastline much easier. The Benagil Cave boat tour cost 20 euros per person and lasted about 45 minutes, departing from Praia de Benagil beach.

The final five days were in Porto. I stayed near the Ribeira district for $55 per night. The Livraria Lello bookstore, often called the most beautiful bookstore in the world, charges a 5-euro entrance fee, which is deducted from any book purchase. Port wine tasting at Graham's cellar cost 18 euros for a flight of four wines.

Total trip cost, including flights, accommodation, food, and activities, came to approximately $2,150 for the full 14 days. The best time I found to visit is either April-May or September-October, avoiding both the summer heat and the winter rains. Temperatures during my visit ranged from 15°C at night to 24°C during the day.

One logistical note: renting the car required an international driving permit, which I obtained through AAA for $20 before departure. Parking in central Porto was surprisingly difficult, so I'd recommend booking accommodation with included parking if driving.
