Complete Care Guide for the Monstera Deliciosa

The Monstera deliciosa, commonly known as the Swiss cheese plant, is native to the tropical forests of southern Mexico and Panama. It typically grows to about 2 meters indoors, though in its natural habitat it can climb up to 20 meters using aerial roots.

Watering should occur every 7-10 days, allowing the top 5 centimeters of soil to dry out between waterings. Overwatering is the most common cause of death for this plant, leading to root rot within 2-3 weeks if left unchecked. During winter months, reduce watering frequency to once every 14 days since growth slows significantly.

Light requirements are moderate to bright indirect light. Direct sunlight for more than 2 hours daily will scorch the leaves, causing brown patches to appear within days. A north or east-facing window is ideal. If leaves aren't developing their characteristic splits (fenestrations), the plant likely needs more light — this typically happens once the plant reaches 3-4 mature leaves.

Temperature should be maintained between 18°C and 27°C. The plant will show stress signals below 13°C, with leaves yellowing and dropping within a week of exposure to cold drafts. Humidity should ideally be above 60%; in drier climates, misting every 2-3 days or using a humidity tray helps prevent crispy leaf edges.

Fertilizing should happen monthly during spring and summer using a balanced liquid fertilizer diluted to half strength. A ratio of 20-20-20 NPK works well. During fall and winter, fertilizing should stop entirely as the plant enters dormancy.

Repotting is necessary every 2 years, or when roots begin emerging from the drainage holes. Choose a pot only 5 centimeters larger in diameter than the current one — too much extra soil retains excess moisture. A well-draining mix of 60% potting soil, 20% perlite, and 20% orchid bark works best.

Common pests include spider mites and mealybugs. Spider mites appear as tiny webs on the underside of leaves and can be treated with neem oil spray every 5 days for three applications. Propagation is straightforward: cut a stem section containing at least one node and aerial root, place in water, and roots typically develop within 3-4 weeks before transferring to soil.
