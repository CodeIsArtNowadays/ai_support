Traditional Ukrainian Borscht Recipe

This recipe serves 8 people and takes approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes total, including 40 minutes of preparation and 1 hour 50 minutes of cooking.

Ingredients required: 500 grams of beef brisket, 3 medium beets, 4 medium potatoes, 1 large onion, 2 carrots, 300 grams of white cabbage, 3 tablespoons of tomato paste, 2 cloves of garlic, 3 liters of water, 2 tablespoons of vinegar, 50 grams of butter, salt, pepper, bay leaves, and fresh dill for garnish.

Begin by placing the beef brisket in a large pot with 3 liters of cold water. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer for 1 hour, skimming foam off the surface every 10-15 minutes. Add 2 bay leaves and 1 teaspoon of salt after the first 20 minutes.

While the meat is cooking, peel and grate the 3 beets. In a separate pan, sauté the grated beets in 30 grams of butter for 8 minutes, then add the tomato paste and 1 tablespoon of vinegar. Cook for an additional 5 minutes, stirring frequently to prevent burning. Set aside.

After the meat has simmered for 1 hour, remove it from the pot and set aside to cool slightly. Add the diced potatoes to the broth and cook for 15 minutes. Then add the shredded cabbage and cook for another 10 minutes.

Dice the onion and carrots, and sauté them in the remaining 20 grams of butter for 6 minutes until softened. Add this mixture to the pot along with the prepared beet mixture. Simmer everything together for 15 minutes.

Cut the cooled beef into bite-sized cubes and return it to the pot. Add 2 minced garlic cloves and simmer for a final 10 minutes. Season with salt and pepper to taste — typically about 1.5 teaspoons of salt and half a teaspoon of black pepper for the full pot.

Turn off the heat and let the borscht rest, covered, for at least 20 minutes before serving — this resting period is essential as it allows the flavors to fully develop. Traditionally, borscht is served with a dollop of sour cream (about 1 tablespoon per bowl) and fresh chopped dill on top. It pairs well with rye bread or garlic-flavored bread called "pampushky."

Leftover borscht keeps well in the refrigerator for up to 4 days and often tastes even better on the second day after the flavors have fully melded together.
