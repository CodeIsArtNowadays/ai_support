Biography of Elena Voskresenskaya (Fictional)

Elena Voskresenskaya was born on March 14, 1987, in the city of Novosibirsk, in what was then the Soviet Union. She was the second of three children born to Mikhail Voskresensky, an engineer at a local factory, and Tatiana Voskresenskaya, a schoolteacher.

At age 7, Elena began studying piano at the Novosibirsk Music School under the guidance of instructor Vera Andreevna Kuznetsova. By age 14, she had won three regional competitions, including the 2001 Siberian Youth Piano Competition, where she performed Chopin's Nocturne in E-flat major.

In 2005, at age 18, Elena moved to Moscow to study at the Moscow Conservatory, majoring in classical piano performance. During her five years there, she studied under Professor Igor Sergeevich Baranov, who had himself studied under a student of Sviatoslav Richter. Her graduation recital in 2010 included Rachmaninoff's Piano Concerto No. 2.

After graduating, Elena spent two years, from 2010 to 2012, performing with regional orchestras across Russia before relocating to Vienna in 2012 to further her career. In Vienna, she joined a chamber music ensemble called the Danube Quartet, performing over 60 concerts across Europe between 2012 and 2016.

In 2016, Elena transitioned into teaching, accepting a position at the Vienna Conservatory of Music. She has taught there for eight years as of 2024, mentoring over 40 students, three of whom have gone on to win international competitions. Her teaching philosophy emphasizes technical precision combined with emotional interpretation, a balance she credits to her early training in Novosibirsk.

Elena married cellist Thomas Brenner in 2014, and the couple has two children, born in 2017 and 2020. She continues to perform occasionally, giving approximately 6-8 solo recitals per year, primarily in Vienna and Salzburg. In 2022, she released her first recorded album, featuring works by Chopin and Rachmaninoff, which sold over 15,000 copies in its first year.

Elena's long-term goal, as she stated in a 2023 interview, is to establish a scholarship fund for young pianists from Siberia who lack access to quality musical education, drawing directly from her own experience growing up far from Russia's major cultural centers.
